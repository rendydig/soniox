HELPY - THE AI ASSISTANT NOTE-TAKER (AND SOON MORE)
====================================================

Helpy is a desktop, always-on-top AI assistant for live two-person
conversations - sales calls, client consultations, interviews, stand-ups,
language practice. It listens to both sides at once, writes the notes you
can't write while you talk, suggests what to say next, and turns the whole
conversation into something you can act on afterwards.

The whole product answers four needs, in this order:

  1. UNDERSTAND the conversation as it happens.
  2. KEEP UP in the moment, in your own voice and language.
  3. REMEMBER everything, so nothing is lost to a bad memory or a busy screen.
  4. FOLLOW UP with a brief, a gap list and a plan instead of a raw transcript.

Every feature below exists to serve one of those needs. Nothing is here
because it was fun to build.


1. UNDERSTAND THE CONVERSATION AS IT HAPPENS
--------------------------------------------

Dual-stream live transcription
  What it does: transcribes two audio sources at the same time - your
  microphone (the Host) and the system audio coming out of your speakers or
  headphones (the Speaker, the person on the other end) - and labels every
  line so you can tell who said what.
  Benefit: you see both halves of the call, not just yourself.
  Why we need it: you cannot listen carefully and type notes at the same
  time; one of the two always loses.

Live translation
  What it does: switch between Live Transcription and Live Translation, type
  a line to translate on demand, and choose the language the assistant should
  answer in.
  Benefit: you can follow - and take part in - a conversation in a language
  you don't speak.
  Why we need it: the interviews and consultations that matter most are
  increasingly cross-language.

AI suggestion window (auto-reply)
  What it does: streams the next thing you should say, word by word, in the
  Speaker's language, using the conversation so far as context.
  Benefit: you get a real-time answer to "what do I say now?" without the
  pause that gives you away.
  Why we need it: dead air is the fastest way to lose a conversation.

Speaker's-move analysis
  What it does: prefixes every suggestion with one plain English line naming
  what the Speaker just did - Assertive, Directive, Commissive, Expressive or
  Declarative - and what they asked, confirmed or stated.
  Benefit: you instantly see the intent behind the last sentence, not just
  its words.
  Why we need it: the right reply depends on what the other person was
  actually trying to do.

Purposes and roles
  What it does: swaps the assistant's persona and objective to fit the
  situation - Language Learning, Casual Networking, Coding Interview, General
  Interview, Consultation - each with the roles you might be playing (for
  example Candidate or Interviewer), plus purposes you write yourself or the
  assistant learns over time.
  Benefit: the suggestion sounds like you in that specific situation.
  Why we need it: a generic chatbot gives generic answers, and generic
  answers lose deals and interviews.

Optional decision layer
  What it does: before writing a reply, a fast "System One" step decides
  whether a reply is even wanted, as whom to speak, which speech act to use
  and in which language.
  Benefit: fewer interruptions and better-timed, in-character suggestions.
  Why we need it: an assistant that answers everything is worse than one
  that knows when to stay quiet.

Pronunciation guide and sample text
  What it does: for language practice, adds a romanized pronunciation hint
  and a sample sentence for the suggested line.
  Benefit: you can actually say the sentence out loud, not just read it.
  Why we need it: a translation you cannot pronounce is useless mid-call.


2. REMEMBER EVERYTHING (THE NOTE-TAKER CORE)
--------------------------------------------

Rolling bullet points
  What it does: keeps a live, AI-maintained list of the important points as
  the conversation moves; only the new lines are sent for each update, exact
  and near-duplicates are merged, the list keeps growing for the whole
  session, and you can pin (lock) any point so a full rebuild can never drop
  or reword it. Runs on a timer, on a hotkey, or on demand, and shows a
  countdown to the next automatic update.
  Benefit: a running list of what actually matters, written while you talk.
  Why we need it: nobody can take good notes and hold a good conversation at
  the same time.

Speaker profile
  What it does: builds a profile of the other person - name, location,
  occupation, plus their interests, goals, pain points and standalone facts -
  using only what the Speaker actually says, never guessed.
  Benefit: you know who you are talking to, and so does the assistant.
  Why we need it: it is the reference the rest of the product reasons
  against; a reply without it is just a plausible sentence.

Last Picked Up
  What it does: after the speaker pauses, gives the latest intent in one or
  two short paragraphs - first in Bahasa Indonesia, then the same content in
  the speaker's own language - and can be triggered on demand at any time.
  Benefit: one glance tells you where the conversation got to.
  Why we need it: attention lapses; you look at a slide, a ticket or a
  message, and you are lost.

Summary
  What it does: one click produces a whole-session summary in fixed sections
  - Overview, Key Points, Decisions, Open Questions, Next Steps, Speaker
  Highlights - written in the conversation's language; you can edit it in
  place and it is saved with the session.
  Benefit: a shareable recap the moment the call ends.
  Why we need it: the value of a meeting is what you carry out of it, and a
  raw transcript carries nothing.

Consulting pipeline
  What it does: three manual steps on one tab - draft a client brief from
  the conversation, edit it and confirm it, then run a gap analysis against
  that confirmed brief, then generate a prioritised execution roadmap with
  every gap folded into a step.
  Benefit: turns a client conversation into a signed-off brief and a plan.
  Why we need it: consulting and business-development calls otherwise
  evaporate into goodwill and no follow-up.

Screenshots to the AI
  What it does: one hotkey captures your screen into a gallery (with a
  zoom/pan viewer), another clears it, and another sends everything captured
  to the assistant with the same persona and conversation history.
  Benefit: ask about a slide, a ticket or a design without typing it out.
  Why we need it: most of the context in a real call lives on the screen,
  not in the words.

Sessions
  What it does: continuously saves the whole conversation - transcript,
  translations, AI results, bullet points, locks, speaker profile,
  screenshots, summary, brief, gaps and roadmap - resumes it when you
  restart, archives it when you start a new one, and shows the elapsed time
  on the Start/Stop button.
  Benefit: nothing is lost to a crash, a stop-and-start or a lunch break.
  Why we need it: a note-taker that loses notes is not a note-taker.


3. STAY OUT OF THE WAY (EXPERIENCE, PRIVACY, CONTROL)
-----------------------------------------------------

Four free-floating panes
  What it does: Live view, AI suggestions, Bullet Points (five tabs: Bullet
  Points, Speaker, Last Picked Up, Summary, Consulting) and Context - each a
  frameless, always-on-top window you can move, resize from any edge, dock to
  either side, and whose layout is remembered for next time.
  Benefit: you put the right information where you can actually see it.
  Why we need it: every call, every screen and every user is a different
  layout.

Pane opacity
  What it does: makes the panes translucent (70% by default, adjustable from
  the menu or a slider) while staying fully readable and clickable.
  Benefit: the app you are presenting or working in stays visible behind
  them.
  Why we need it: an opaque panel sitting on top of your slide deck is worse
  than no panel at all.

Context pane
  What it does: edits the Host and Speaker profile files right inside the
  app, with live reload if you edit them in an external editor and atomic
  autosave while you type.
  Benefit: the assistant knows who is in the room, by name and by role.
  Why we need it: a name and a job title change the right answer.

Screen Protection
  What it does: hides the app's own windows from screen capture and screen
  sharing while keeping them visible to you.
  Benefit: the assistant stays invisible to the other party.
  Why we need it: the tool is only useful if it is discreet.

Soft microphone mute
  What it does: mutes only your microphone, keeps the other stream running
  with keepalives, and resumes instantly with no reconnect.
  Benefit: you can go off-record for a moment without ending the session.
  Why we need it: private asides happen in every real call.

Configurable global hotkeys
  What it does: system-wide shortcuts for capture, clear and send
  screenshots, manual bullet points, manual Last Picked Up, hiding every
  pane at once, and the Context pane - all rebindable in Settings.
  Benefit: full control without leaving the window you are working in.
  Why we need it: you are never inside the app while you are talking.

Web monitor
  What it does: serves the same live view to any browser on the machine, plus
  a dedicated suggestions-only page.
  Benefit: a second screen, or a teammate, can watch the same live feed.
  Why we need it: one screen is not always enough.


4. WHY IT DOESN'T FALL OVER
---------------------------

Crash-isolated audio capture
  What it does: runs all audio capture in a separate child process that is
  restarted automatically if it dies, with a bounded retry limit.
  Benefit: a long call never takes the whole app down with it.
  Why we need it: the audio stack can fault natively in real hardware setups,
  and that kind of crash cannot be caught inside the app.

Central hub
  What it does: a small companion process owns the live session state and the
  message bus - ordered events per topic, an append-only event log, atomic
  snapshots, and a small debug endpoint.
  Benefit: every pane, and soon every separate agent, reads one ordered,
  subscribable source of truth.
  Why we need it: it is the foundation the next set of features is built on.

Diagnostics
  What it does: logs crashes and native faults to disk, samples health every
  30 seconds (memory, threads, note volume, per-pane browser memory), and
  ships a stress harness that drives the real app with real audio for long
  runs.
  Benefit: multi-hour sessions stay flat instead of slowly degrading.
  Why we need it: this app is meant to be left running all day.

Windows and macOS, properly
  What it does: handles each platform's realities - output loopback capture
  on Windows, virtual-device system audio on macOS, native always-on-top,
  and a screen-recording permission check before the first screenshot.
  Benefit: the same product, working, on either machine.
  Why we need it: the people who need this most work across both.


5. AND SOON
-----------

Helpy is a note-taker today. The next steps build on the central hub:

  - Panes read their state from the hub instead of each holding a copy.
  - The assistant's helpers (notes, summary, consulting) run as separate,
    supervised processes rather than inside the app.
  - All timing - debounce, cadence, retries - is owned by the hub, so the
    assistant behaves the same no matter which part is running.
  - The hub becomes the single source of truth for saved sessions.
  - The web server is folded in, so there is one process to run, not three.

The direction is simple: from a note-taker that helps you during a call, to
an assistant that runs the whole loop around it - before, during and after.
